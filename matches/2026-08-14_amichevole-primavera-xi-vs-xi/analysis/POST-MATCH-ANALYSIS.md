# Post-match analysis — Napoli Blu vs Napoli Verde

**Match:** Amichevole Primavera XI vs XI  
**Date:** 14 August 2026  
**Official score (tape):** 3–1 Blu (Saviano 9', Saviano 30'; Anic 2H 1'; Mané 2H 14')  
**Analyst note:** Roughly seven goals were scored across both sides in ~60'; three of those were offside. Chance volume was high either way.

---

## Overall verdict

A strong internal test. Defensively the group was generally solid; in possession the midfield dictated play and progressed the ball well. Number 10s and strikers showed clear growth in positioning and off-ball movement. The next step is communication, scanning, and taking the right risk at the right moment — especially wing-backs on the ball, midfielders in transition, and strikers’ timing vs the line.

---

## Key strengths

1. **No. 10s — positioning & off-ball movement (esp. in build-up)**  
   More creative off the ball, more direct and insistent in runs. Anic found pockets, then quickly attacked channels with strong match effectiveness. Rovetini showed the same idea, with less end product on the day.

2. **Strikers staying high — Saviano & Mané**  
   Presence on the last line (rather than always dropping) created an extra layer of space for the 10s. Good understanding of where to stand and where to move into during build-up.

3. **Progression with fewer, better passes**  
   Technique, weight and direction of passes impressed. Branchizio was central: diagonal, direct balls into the striker / 10s — exactly what the coach is looking for. He also delays well (sole of the boot) to scan, attract pressure, then play.

4. **Organised build-up & line-breaking**  
   In structured phases they broke lines well. Defenders were often the main directors of play — fluid and direct. All recorded goals came from build-up situations.

5. **Chance creation & goal threat**  
   Many scoring opportunities between both sides; volume says a lot even when some finishes were offside.

6. **High defence — trigger presses (esp. in transition)**  
   Better understood. Blu had a strong spell after losing the ball: kept Verde at bay, forced errors, pinned play in their third.

7. **Mid-block**  
   Composed. Side-to-side shifting well executed; consistency carried over from the Portici game.

8. **Transitions — when to counter vs consolidate**  
   Clearer understanding of when to go and when to hold shape.

9. **GK / outfield communication (out of possession)**  
   More vocal. Magliano showed early improvement; that habit needs to be kept up.

10. **Wing-backs off the ball**  
    Among the hardest-working players in the group — engines without the ball.

---

## Key weaknesses / development areas

1. **Attacking communication (esp. striker ↔ 10s)**  
   Example (1H): Saviano drops deep, Anic cuts across the same line, ball scrambles, chance lost. Simple calls (“mine”, “Anic’s ball”) matter — don’t run blindly and hope.

2. **Offside timing (Saviano & Mané)**  
   Too often ahead of the line — body shape, or defenders stepping up smartly. Reduce offside “goals” and turn them into official ones.

3. **Transition / midfield — risk & scanning**  
   At times midfielders preferred the easy / vertical recycle over switching play to change the game.  
   Example: Chiummariello wins it — options to De Stefano (free, other side / outside peripheral vision) vs easy ball to Zappalá expecting a recycle. Chose the safer option; need more scan + surprise.

4. **High line after build-up pressure**  
   When about to lose the ball in build-up, still push up and suffocate space. One moment they didn’t: Verde played through and created a chance.

5. **Counter-attack directness**  
   When the decision is to go, be more risk-taking and direct (same theme as the Chiummariello / De Stefano example). Goals came from build-up, not from those transition moments.

6. **GK distribution — Spinelli (esp. 1H)**  
   Often preferred the easy option rather than seeing further / playing more direct. Vs man-oriented press: Saviano is tall, physical, flair — use him (e.g. longer ball into him when he’s 1v1).

7. **Wing-backs on the ball — decisions & prep**  
   Off the ball excellent; on the ball unclear (skill? go wide? drive?). Creativity and pre-receive scanning are the next step.  
   - **Melnyk:** 1v1, chance to drive / early or low cross for Saviano attacking the box — instead tucked back safe to a midfielder not set for a shot. If Saviano doesn’t attack the space, Melnyk should still drive into it rather than close the body.  
   - **Zappalá:** Received in open space, cut inside diagonally instead of progressing; could have forced the opposite wing-back (Chiaiese) to step or drop, buying time to find a 10 between the lines.

8. **Spinelli — more onto the play**  
   Needs to be more direct and onto players, especially after mistakes.

## What went well (WWB) — ready for match tab

Short bullets for `reflection.wentWell`:

1. No. 10s (Anic especially; Rovetini same idea) — pockets of space, channels, creative insisting runs in build-up.  
2. Saviano & Mané holding the line created space for the 10s; strong positioning / off-ball connection developing.  
3. Progression with fewer, quality passes; Branchizio’s diagonal balls into 9/10s and his delay-to-scan habit.  
4. Organised build-up broke lines; defenders dictated fluid/direct play; all goals from build-up.  
5. High volume of chances / goal threat across the game.  
6. High-defence triggers clearer in transition; good Blu spell pinning Verde and forcing errors.  
7. Better feel for when to counter vs consolidate shape.  
8. GK–team communication out of possession improving (Magliano early signs).

---

## Even better if (EBI) — ready for match tab

Short bullets for `reflection.evenBetterIf`:

1. Clearer attacking communication (striker ↔ 10s): call the ball so drops and runs don’t collide (Saviano / Anic 1H example).  
2. Better timing / body shape vs the line from Saviano & Mané — fewer offsides, more legal goals.  
3. In transition, scan and take the surprise switch (e.g. Chiummariello → De Stefano free) instead of always recycling the easy vertical.  
4. When build-up is about to break, keep the line high and squeeze space so the opponent can’t play through.  
5. When countering, more direct risk-taking — same standard as organised build-up.  
6. Spinelli: see further, play more direct vs man-to-man (use Saviano’s physical profile); stay more onto players after errors.  
7. Wing-backs on the ball: decide earlier (drive, cross, isolate) — Melnyk drive/cross vs tuck-back; Zappalá progress vs early cut-inside.
8. Set pieces — clearer ideas and rehearsed routines for more efficiency; prepare strategies for the next friendly.
